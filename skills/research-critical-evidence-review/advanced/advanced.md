# Method-specific extension

## Applied method

1. Restate the claim and separate descriptive, associational, predictive, and causal forms.
2. Identify the unit of evidence and study design; inspect sampling, comparator, measurement, missingness, confounding, outcome selection, analysis, and reporting.
3. Choose an appraisal framework appropriate to that design and the requested decision; do not apply one universal grade.
4. Check whether effect estimates, uncertainty, robustness, replication, and contrary evidence support the claim.
5. Distinguish evidence about the sample from generalization to a target population and report what cannot be established.
6. Give a calibrated conclusion with specific reasons, strongest alternative explanation, and next evidence that would change it.

## Focused support: appraisal checks

1. Describe the claim and its evidence unit; identify the study design, population, comparator, exposure/intervention, outcome, measurement, and analysis before judging strength.
2. Choose a risk-of-bias instrument that fits that design and question. Do not apply a single evidence ladder or grade to randomized trials, observational studies, diagnostic studies, qualitative work, or mechanistic evidence indiscriminately.
3. Check recruitment and selection, baseline comparability, confounding, missingness, measurement error, outcome switching, selective reporting, multiplicity, model assumptions, and whether the analysis preserves the design. For experimental claims, inspect allocation, blinding where relevant, adherence, attrition, and protocol deviations.
4. Inspect estimate size and uncertainty, robustness, replication, external validity, and contradictory evidence. A small p-value or a single study does not by itself establish importance or causation.
5. Test reasoning for post hoc inference, base-rate neglect, survivorship/selection bias, ecological fallacy, false dichotomy, appeal to authority, and conflating absence of evidence with evidence of absence. Name the concrete inferential step at issue rather than labeling a person or source.
6. Report which observations are direct, which conclusions are inference, what alternative explanation is strongest, and what evidence could change the conclusion. Do not emit a universal numeric certainty score.

## Scope guard

Preserve the assigned research question and route domain-owner decisions to the accountable specialist.

If the requested method cannot be completed with available evidence or tools, return a bounded partial deliverable and name the precise gap. Do not replace a missing input with an invented value.
