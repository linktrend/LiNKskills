# Method-specific extension

## Applied method

1. Classify the clinical question and study phase; identify who has authority to approve clinical decisions.
2. Define target population, intervention, comparator, endpoint, estimand, follow-up, and design before calculating sample size.
3. Select a design-appropriate endpoint and analysis; flag surrogate outcomes, multiplicity, censoring, missingness, and competing risks.
4. Calculate scenarios only from supplied or explicitly sourced assumptions; show methods and uncertainty; do not treat estimates as guarantees.
5. Assess recruitment, retention, sites, operational burden, safety monitoring, ethics, and data feasibility.
6. Produce a draft synopsis and risk list with named clinical/biostatistical/regulatory reviewer.
7. Stop short of a finished protocol, treatment advice, ethics submission, or regulatory determination.

## Focused support: study design, endpoints, and trial operations

### Study design selection

Choose the design from the question and the intervention assignment unit. For intervention effects, state whether allocation is randomized, clustered, crossover, or observational; for descriptive, diagnostic, prognostic, or feasibility questions, use a design and estimand that answer that question. State the population, comparator, time origin, follow-up, analysis population, and material sources of confounding or selection. Do not call an observational comparison a trial.

### Endpoint and power planning

Name one primary endpoint and distinguish secondary, exploratory, safety, and surrogate endpoints. Define its measurement, timepoint, estimand, and clinically meaningful difference before power calculation. Select a calculation appropriate to the endpoint and design, and show assumed variance or event rate, alpha, target power, attrition, clustering, and multiplicity adjustments where applicable. If a material assumption lacks evidence, give sensitivity scenarios or mark sample size uncomputed; never substitute an invented rate.

### Trial feasibility and operations

Check eligibility, recruitment funnel, retention, site capacity, intervention fidelity, data availability, safety monitoring, and follow-up burden against the proposed design. Record operational dependencies and identify the clinician, biostatistician, regulatory, ethics, and site owners. Use a synopsis outline only as a planning aid; the applicable protocol and ethics templates are owner-controlled. Return decisions requiring clinical or regulatory authority to those owners.

## Scope guard

This is planning support only. A qualified clinician and biostatistician own clinical design and interpretation; any regulatory or ethics determination remains with the accountable specialist.

If the requested method cannot be completed with available evidence or tools, return a bounded partial deliverable and name the precise gap. Do not replace a missing input with an invented value.
