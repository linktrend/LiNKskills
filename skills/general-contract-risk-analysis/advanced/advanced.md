# General contract risk analysis cards

## Trigger and boundaries

Use when a requester wants a substantive risk scan of a contract but supplies no applicable organizational playbook. This task differs from playbook review: do not turn general issue-spotting into an invented company position, market standard, risk threshold or legal opinion.

## Review sequence

1. Confirm agreement version, contract type, represented party, counterparty, negotiation/execution status, purpose, deadline and focus. If party is unknown, describe both-side effects and ask for role before directional redlines.
2. Read the full document; inventory pages, blanks, undefined terms, missing or unprovided referenced exhibits, signature blocks and amendment precedence.
3. Extract key terms and locate section evidence for payment/price changes, term/renewal, termination, liability limits/carveouts, indemnity/claim process, confidentiality, IP, data/privacy/security, warranties, service levels, assignment, governing-law/forum, insurance, audit, force majeure and type-specific provisions. Report absent, present, not applicable or unclear.
4. Inspect clause interactions and internal consistency, including carveouts against caps, survival after termination, data return, conflicting precedence, undefined/cross-referenced terms and missing schedules.
5. For each issue, state the clause/evidence, who bears the obligation or exposure, possible business/legal impact, factual assumptions, confidence and what authority or playbook is missing. Use descriptive low/medium/high labels only when their rationale is explained; avoid numerical risk scores unless the requester supplied a scale.
6. Offer draft wording or negotiation options only where useful. Label any general commercial observation as an unverified hypothesis unless supported by a supplied/current authoritative source; do not present sample benchmark figures in the original source as universal current law or market facts.
7. Return executive summary, key-term table, issue list ranked by rationale, missing provisions, inconsistencies, questions, and recommended owner/counsel review.

## Same-task addition: agreement-version comparison

When the request supplies multiple contract versions, first identify each file/version/date and any order-of-precedence language. Compare clause by clause; report additions, removals, changed wording, and material cross-clause effects with pinpoints to both versions. Keep a changed text fact separate from its risk inference and negotiation option. Do not label the newest copy controlling unless the contract or owner evidence establishes that status.
