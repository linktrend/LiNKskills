Formal vs. Informal Onboarding

Companies can use formal or informal onboarding techniques to help new hires adjust to their role and perform well. Formal onboarding follows a set plan with training and checklists, while informal onboarding is flexible, using team tasks and support to teach.

Both onboarding plan types have key benefits:

  • Formal onboarding uses a clear plan with steps, guides, and set training.
  • Informal onboarding relies on real-world work, peer help, and hands-on learning.
  • Formal methods ensure full training, fewer mistakes, and clear role understanding.
  • Informal methods help employees adapt fast and learn through daily work.
  • Both styles help new hires adjust, grow, and succeed. A well-balanced onboarding plan blends structure and hands-on learning for the best results.
     

Choosing the right mix depends on company goals, culture, and job needs.

Onboarding Best Practices

An onboarding process helps new hires adjust quickly, feel engaged, and perform well. The best programs mix clear steps, team support, and tasks. Companies that follow onboarding best practices give employees the tools to grow and succeed.

Here are some best practices for a successful onboarding program:

  • Start early by sending a welcome email and preboarding tasks before the first day.
  • Set clear goals so that new hires know their role, team, and company mission.
  • Use a structured plan with training, checklists, and regular check-ins.
  • Encourage team support with mentors, peer guidance, and networking.
  • Provide real tasks early to build confidence and hands-on learning.
     

A strong onboarding plan blends structure, training, and team support. When done right, it helps new hires grow fast, stay engaged, and drive company success.
