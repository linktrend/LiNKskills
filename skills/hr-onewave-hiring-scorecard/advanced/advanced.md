# Structured Hiring Scorecard Design: task method and acceptance

## Trigger and task edge

Use when creating a role-specific interviewer scorecard with observable competencies, anchored ratings, consistent questions and a debrief guide.

The single-task result is: Competency matrix and observable behavioral anchors. Separate adjacent work stays routed to its own task.

## Required evidence

- Approved role and job description with essential outcomes
- Must-have and preferred criteria supplied by hiring owner
- Level, interview stages, panel roles and interview time
- Approved rating scale or request to draft a proposed scale

## Procedure and branch notes

1. Confirm role, level, job outcomes, interview stages and owner. If the requisition or criteria are not approved, label the scorecard a draft proposal.
2. Translate each must-have outcome into specific observable evidence and a verification method. Separate essential criteria from preferences and remove vague “culture fit” or personality proxies.
3. Select a role-appropriate set of competencies; use only those relevant to job outcomes. Define each competency, evidence examples and what a lower/higher anchored rating means. If the source 1–5 scale is used, label it proposed until owner adoption.
4. Write consistent behavioral/situational questions per competency with follow-up probes and evidence indicators. Avoid questions about protected/personal traits and unsupported assumptions about candidate background.
5. Create an independent interviewer scoresheet with evidence notes, rating, confidence/coverage and missing evidence. Do not aggregate into a hire/reject score without approved weighting and decision rule.
6. Add debrief instructions that gather evidence before opinions, compare candidates against same role criteria, record dissent, and separate concerns requiring verification. Include bias checks as prompts, not proof.
7. Provide reference-check questions only where authorized and lawful. Prepare one panel-ready draft with source/criteria and owner approval questions; do not contact candidates or decide selection.

## Acceptance checks

- Every material input and rule has an evidence reference or is labeled an assumption/open question.
- Calculations show formula, denominator, units, population and source date; arithmetic is independently rechecked.
- Findings distinguish observed facts, attributed statements, hypotheses, assumptions and owner decisions.
- Missing evidence blocks only the conclusion that depends on it; complete unaffected sections.
- Personal data is minimized and any small-group disclosure is suppressed under the supplied privacy rule.
- Drafts contain no unsupported legal, company-policy, approval, employment or fairness conclusion.
- No external effect, message, system mutation, filing, signature or business decision occurs.

## Jurisdiction, evidence and escalation

Do not assume a jurisdiction, entity, employment classification, policy, reporting standard or legal duty. For current law, verify official primary authorities for the actual location/date and route interpretation to qualified counsel. Treat secondary articles and source examples as research leads only. Preserve date, record ID, source scope and contradictory evidence. Escalate only material safety, legal, retaliation, privacy or decision-right issues; routine drafts continue within scope.
