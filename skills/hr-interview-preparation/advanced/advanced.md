# Interview Plan and Scorecard Method

## Card A — Role and competency map

Create a source table with role requirement, source and date, observed work outcome, competency, assessment evidence and interviewer. Convert vague requirements (“culture fit,” “strong communicator”) into observable behaviors connected to actual job tasks. Select 4–6 non-overlapping competencies, prioritizing requirements that are both important and assessable. Mark unsupported competencies as proposed and request the role owner’s confirmation; do not silently alter approved criteria.

## Card B — Structured question bank

For every competency draft 2–3 behavioral and 1–2 situational questions. Tag each question with the specific evidence it elicits. Use consistent core prompts across candidates in the same stage. Keep follow-ups neutral: context, individual contribution, alternatives considered, result, and learning. Do not ask leading questions that reveal a preferred answer. Provide equivalent prompts when a candidate lacks a particular industry or employer background.

## Card C — Panel and timing

Build an agenda that fits the actual interview duration: introductions and role context; competency segments; candidate questions; close. Assign each competency to an interviewer based on relevant expertise and avoid panel duplication. Note the evidence expected, interviewer, question IDs, time and handoff. If the panel cannot assess an important competency, flag the gap rather than imply complete coverage.

## Card D — Anchored scoring

Create a competency-specific 1–4 rubric. Common anchors: 1 no relevant evidence or clear gap; 2 partial evidence with material gaps; 3 specific evidence meeting the stated bar; 4 repeated, high-impact evidence exceeding the stated bar. Customize each anchor using role outcomes. Define “not assessed” separately from 1. Record evidence and interviewer confidence; do not convert “not assessed” to zero. Do not average scores into an automatic selection rule.

## Card E — Debrief and evidence discipline

Ask interviewers to complete notes and scores independently before discussion. For each competency, summarize behavior and result with a short evidence quote/paraphrase and question ID. Resolve disagreement by comparing evidence against the role bar, not seniority or intuition. Record additional evidence needed, dissent, and accountable decision owner. Do not include irrelevant personal attributes or speculative personality labels.

## Deliverable checklist

Role/source summary; competencies and mapping; interview schedule; question bank and probes; 1–4 anchored scorecard plus not-assessed field; interviewer assignments; debrief form; assumptions; open role decisions; privacy note. If a requested element depends on an unavailable source, leave an explicit placeholder and complete the rest.
