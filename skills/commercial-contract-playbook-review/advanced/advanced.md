# Playbook contract review cards

## Trigger and intake

Use for an existing commercial agreement when a negotiation playbook exists and the requester wants review, issue ranking, or draft redlines. Confirm document identity/version/execution state, contract type, client party/role, counterparty, deadline, business objective, focus clauses, and the relevant playbook/version. If party or playbook scope is unclear, ask only what changes the direction of the review; continue separable document completeness checks.

## Review sequence

1. **Completeness:** read all supplied pages and referenced exhibits; identify drafts/executed status, blanks, placeholders, missing schedules, truncation, and defined terms used inconsistently.
2. **Position and playbook:** map the party represented and transaction context. Extract each applicable playbook provision verbatim as source evidence plus date/owner/status. Distinguish adopted positions, ranges, fallback language, escalation rules, and open proposals. Never invent an organization position.
3. **Clause inventory:** check limitation of liability and carveouts, indemnity and claim control, IP ownership/licenses, privacy/security/data processing, confidentiality, warranties, term/renewal/termination, payment/price change, assignment/change of control, insurance, force majeure, service levels/support, audit rights, dispute law/forum, and contract-type-specific obligations. Record present/absent/not applicable/unclear with clause citation.
4. **Interaction checks:** test how caps interact with indemnities and exclusions; how termination interacts with data return, transition, accrued fees and survival; whether defined terms/exhibits cross-reference; and whether a favorable clause is neutralized elsewhere.
5. **Issue analysis:** for each playbook departure, state section/quoted evidence, playbook source/position, factual or legal uncertainty, business impact by represented party, severity rationale, and whether the clause is negotiable under the playbook. A legal-law conclusion requires jurisdiction, governing text, date and current primary authority; otherwise label unresolved.
6. **Draft alternatives:** propose minimal redline language as a review draft, linked to the specific clause and playbook. Give a preferred ask plus fallback only when a documented fallback exists; otherwise propose alternatives as options and label them unapproved. Identify downstream negotiation tradeoffs.
7. **Prioritize:** rank issues against stated deadline, playbook escalation gates, deal objective and dependencies. Do not use source-specific numeric market heuristics as universal thresholds.
8. **Deliver:** executive summary; document completeness; key terms; clause table; issue/risk analysis; exact proposed redlines; options/fallback status; missing terms/exhibits; internal inconsistencies; open facts/law; owner/counsel review points.

## Merged method variants

The active workflow uses KWP playbook loading and whole-agreement clause/interdependency review as the base. Borghei’s source adds a fast red-flag pass, explicit party/deal leverage intake, risk/priority tiers and structured fallback comparison; use these as prompts, not fixed severity thresholds. Original scripts and source-specific GREEN/YELLOW/RED conventions are retained but not executed or adopted as signature clearance. The Lawve playbook reviewer original is preserved unchanged; its protected language is not adapted into the active method.

## Authority boundary

Provide evidence-grounded substantive legal issue analysis and redline options as drafts. No signing, sending, CLM write, negotiation communication, waiver, or claim that a document is legally safe. Sara can prepare routine analysis; counsel/Carlos reviews consequential legal conclusions or external action.
