# Method-specific extension

## Applied method

1. Define the market, buyer, geography, unit, period, and decision; separate fact from assumption.
2. Gather current primary and reputable secondary sources for market drivers and competitor claims; date every material number.
3. Estimate market using bottom-up customer/unit economics and top-down external totals where data permits; reconcile differences rather than average them blindly.
4. For survey work, specify population/frame, question wording, sampling method, expected response, segments, and uncertainty; distinguish representativeness from sample size alone.
5. Evaluate candidate segments using measurable demand, accessibility, economic viability, differentiation, and actionability, supporting scores with evidence.
6. Compare competitors only on aligned definitions and periods; mark estimates and missing data.
7. Report range/scenarios and what new evidence would change the decision; do not produce precision unsupported by inputs.

## Focused support: market framework, survey, and competitor methods

### Select a market lens

Define buyer, need, product boundary, geography, unit, and period first. Use top-down totals and bottom-up customer or unit economics as independent estimates, reconcile differing denominators, and report ranges with source dates. TAM/SAM/SOM labels are useful only when market boundaries and reachable segments are explicit; do not divide unrelated market totals into false precision. Use a competitive-forces or positioning lens only when it answers the decision and evidence supports each factor; avoid unsupported scores.

### Survey and segment evidence

Specify the target population and sampling frame, recruitment method, question wording, field period, response and nonresponse, weighting, segment definitions, and intended inference. Check coverage, self-selection, duplicate responses, leading questions, and missing data. Report uncertainty only when the design and sample support it; sample size alone does not prove representativeness. For interviews or small convenience samples, report themes and selection limits rather than population percentages.

### Competitor comparisons

Compare like with like: same period, currency, revenue basis, customer unit, and geography. Distinguish company-reported facts, third-party estimates, and inference; normalize only when the underlying definitions are available. A gap in public data is an unknown, not a zero or an opportunity score.

## Scope guard

This produces evidence and assumptions for a business decision; it does not decide pricing, investment, campaign execution, or GTM strategy.

If the requested method cannot be completed with available evidence or tools, return a bounded partial deliverable and name the precise gap. Do not replace a missing input with an invented value.
