# Sector, theme, and cross-asset opportunity research: successor proposal v0.1.1

Status: isolated source-completion successor proposal, not canonical, enabled, or qualified. It replaces seven generic source placeholders. Approved group 5 is “Sector, thematic and cross-asset opportunity research.” The frozen method can produce qualitative sector research, but must distinguish descriptive opportunity synthesis from a vendor-defined numeric rotation/theme score.

## Required task contract

Inputs: question/horizon, geography, sector/theme/universe definition and as-of membership; supplied or accessible primary-source company, industry, market and cross-asset observations; each source date, period, unit/currency, geography, denominator, classification, transformations, and data rights; instrument/benchmark and price basis where relative performance is discussed; value-chain assertions with source and coverage; and requested deliverable/tool availability. For numeric comparisons, require comparable members, constituent history, classification version, measurement period and missing coverage. Unknown categories or taxonomy mappings remain explicit.

Outputs: dated sector/theme map; value-chain roles and cited issuer examples; driver and counterdriver ledger; supported cross-asset links with exact instrument/contract, currency, geography, basis and period; current catalyst/scenario view; optional quantitative comparison only where data and definitions are supplied; limitations and next evidence. Separate sourced fact, management/market claim, inference and hypothesis. No universal score, investable ranking, ETF/sector exposure or causal relation by default.

## Integrated method

1. **Set the perimeter.** Define the sector/theme in words and, when needed, attach a named classification version and included/excluded entities. Record geography, horizon, benchmark, date, and whether the unit is company, industry, supply chain, commodity, rate, FX or security. “AI”, “energy transition” or “defense” is not a stable universe without a declared scope. Preserve aliases and contested category membership.

2. **Map the value chain.** Identify suppliers, infrastructure/enablers, producers, distributors, end users and substitutes using filings, regulator/industry sources and supplied company evidence. For each node provide example entities only where the evidence verifies actual economic exposure; distinguish direct revenue from narrative association. Record concentration, bottlenecks, substitutability, capacity, inventories, lead times, pricing power and regulatory/technology dependencies when evidence supports them. No investment benefit is inferred from theme membership alone.

3. **Build the driver ledger.** For each driver record direction, source, release/as-of, geography, measured value/unit, period, comparison baseline and whether observed or forecast. Examples include supply/demand balances, capacity additions, cost inputs, policy, rates, FX, credit and adoption. Mark overlapping drivers so they are not double-counted. Show evidence against the theme and alternative explanation (cyclical rebound, inventory swing, base effect, currency or broad-market move).

4. **Connect cross-asset exposures.** State a directional mechanism as a hypothesis: which price/input, who bears it, transmission lag, hedge/contract basis, currency, and observable falsifier. Compare the asset and benchmark over aligned windows only when actual source series with contract/venue and as-of are provided. Separate spot, front future, curve, ETF and issuer equity; do not treat them as interchangeable. A cross-asset relationship is not “correlation” without paired observations and a stated statistic, nor causal without design.

5. **Compare participants and opportunity conditions.** Compare issuers on sourced business exposure, margins/capacity, balance sheet/funding, valuation inputs, catalysts, liquidity and downside. Units and periods must align; explain classification/FX/restatement differences. If proprietary taxonomy or private market-share data are unavailable, keep claims qualitative and label the missing rows. No opaque rank or conviction score.

6. **Optional quantitative sector rotation.** Only if a user asks and a point-in-time universe, classification, security returns, weighting convention, rebalance dates, dividends/corporate actions and FX are supplied, calculate per-sector period total return r_s = (adjusted_value_end / adjusted_value_start) − 1 and named-benchmark return r_b on identical endpoints; define relative arithmetic return as r_s − r_b in percentage points. State split/dividend/FX adjustments and whether price or total-return series is used. Calculate breadth as a separate numerator/denominator ratio. Show source/coverage and equal-weight/market-weight choice. Do not adopt source-specific fixed thresholds, score weights, regional ETF catalogs or historical performance claims. Report the raw table and caveats before offering any advisory conclusion.

7. **Report.** Provide thesis, scope, map, supporting/counterevidence, measured data, source-timed catalysts, scenario paths, potentially affected assets, valuation/implementation dependencies and missing evidence. State what would change the thesis. Jane may recommend sector/asset choices and increase/trim/exit or target/risk levels when evidence, holdings and constraints support them; those proposals are advisory. No order, live activation, policy mutation or new broker/adapter. Any material/live/capital change requires Lisa and Carlos independent approval channels for the exact version.
