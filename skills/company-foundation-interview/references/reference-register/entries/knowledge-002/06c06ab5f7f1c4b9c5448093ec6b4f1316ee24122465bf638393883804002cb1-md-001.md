### ASQ — public quality learning directory

- Publisher/source: American Society for Quality, [Learn About Quality](https://asq.org/quality-resources/learn-about-quality), [Process Analysis Tools](https://asq.org/quality-resources/process-analysis-tools), [Flowchart](https://asq.org/quality-resources/flowchart), [Control Chart](https://asq.org/quality-resources/control-chart), [SIPOC+CM](https://asq.org/quality-resources/sipoc), and [Five Whys and Five Hows](https://asq.org/quality-resources/five-whys).
- Classification: maintained professional-association guidance and methods pages; not a normative standard. The public web reader returned readable topic and method text on 2026-10-05 even though the earlier native fetch returned 403. The flowchart page identifies itself as reviewed June 2025 and cites ASQ Quality Press reference material.
- Edition/publication date: no fixed edition/date exposed; retrieve date 2026-10-05. Refresh by checking the current page before use.
- Captured method content: ASQ’s flowchart procedure defines the process and boundaries, lists activities, orders them, draws flow arrows after participants agree on sequence, and validates with process workers/supervisors/suppliers/customers by walking through the work. Its review checklist covers unclear steps, bottlenecks, delays, errors/rework, people/knowledge/authority gaps, duplication, handoffs, cycle time and unnecessary steps; it says to map current state. The process-analysis page also explains FMEA as identifying failure modes, studying effects and prioritizing reduction. ASQ’s control-chart procedure selects a chart for the data, chooses a collection period, plots and analyzes time-ordered data, investigates and documents out-of-control signals, keeps monitoring, and recalculates limits only after at least 20 sequential in-control points. These pages also distinguish common from special causes; control charts need suitable ordered data and competent interpretation. SIPOC+CM and Five Whys are maintained additional routes, but are not required for the minimum flowchart/control-chart method.
- Useful for pre-interview questions: What process is being improved, where does it start/end, who performs and receives each handoff, where are delays/errors/rework, and what time-ordered evidence exists? First document current state with participants; only consider a control chart if data type, volume and process context support it. Treat methods as optional tools; do not presume a formal QMS, certification objective, or manufacturing-style control regime.
- Full normative standard: not applicable; no normative text is claimed. ASQ pages opened and read by the public web reader are a sufficient maintained authoritative route for initial factual interviews and a basic current-state flowchart procedure; actual source page content is summarized in this capture. Use a separate statistical source/competent reviewer before applying control limits to consequential decisions.

- Publisher/source: International Organization for Standardization (ISO), [ISO 9000 family](https://www.iso.org/standards/popular/iso-9000-family), [ISO 9000:2026](https://committee.iso.org/standard/9000?browse=tc), [ISO 9001:2026](https://committee.iso.org/standard/9001), [ISO 9001 made simple (free brochure)](https://www.iso.org/publication/PUB100368.html), and [How to use it: ISO 9001:2026 (free brochure)](https://www.iso.org/publication/PUB100373.html).
- Classification: voluntary international standards plus ISO-published public guidance. The official 9000 family page is an overview, not the standards text.
- Current editions checked 2026-10-05: ISO 9000:2026, edition 5, published 2026-05, 53 pages; ISO 9001:2026, edition 6, published 2026-09-16, replacing ISO 9001:2015. ISO's public catalogue lists the standards for purchase; exact normative full texts were not acquired. The 2026 brochure pages list “ISO 9001 made simple” (edition 2) and “How to use it” (edition 3) as free/CHF 0. Their pages expressly prohibit use of ISO content for AI prompting or enabling AI responses except ISO Open Data. Because the requested destination is Sara, the brochure files were not downloaded or extracted into this source pack. The public Download links resolve to readable PDF items in the web reader (PUB100368: 16 pages; PUB100373: 20 pages), but ordinary native curl of each exact official PDF URL returned HTTP 403. No local source bytes/digests were obtained or workaround attempted. The free route is verified; the web-reader page is readable, but this pack does not provide brochure text for Sara because of the stated use limit.
- Captured text/facts: ISO defines ISO 9000 as quality-management fundamentals/vocabulary, while ISO 9001 provides requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and improving a QMS. ISO states certification to ISO 9001 is optional. The public family page describes customer focus, leadership engagement, process approach and continual improvement as principles.
- Useful for pre-interview questions: What customer/product/service requirements govern delivery? What processes and owners are defined? Where are acceptance/complaint/correction records? How are supplier inputs controlled? What metrics show process effectiveness and improvement? Has anyone asked for certification or a contractual conformity claim? ### ISO 31000 — risk guidance

- Publisher/source: ISO, [ISO 31000:2018 record and preview](https://www.iso.org/standard/65694.html); current public committee information at [ISO/TC 262](https://committee.iso.org/sites/tc262/home/projects/published/iso-31000-2018-risk-management.html); free [ISO 31000 brochure](https://www.iso.org/publication/PUB100426.html).
- Classification: voluntary risk-management guideline, not law. ISO 31000:2018, edition 2, publication 2018-02; official page states it was reviewed/confirmed in 2023 and remains current, while stage 90.92 says to be revised and a successor is under development. Catalogue shows 16 pages and a CHF 135 purchase price. Full standard not acquired.
- Captured text/facts: official preview says the standard provides principles/guidelines and covers identifying, analyzing, evaluating, treating, monitoring and communicating risk. ISO explicitly says it is not certifiable.
- Useful interview procedure: for each material objective, elicit uncertainty/event, cause, consequence, existing controls, likelihood/evidence, impact, owner, response, trigger, residual risk and review date; label facts separately from hypotheses. Do not treat a scoring matrix as a legal/compliance determination.
- Limits: official preview/brochure only, not normative text. No ISO 31000 certification claim.

### ISO 22301 — business-continuity requirements

- Publisher/source: ISO, [ISO 22301:2019 record and preview](https://www.iso.org/standard/75106.html).
- Classification: voluntary international management-system standard; not presumed mandatory for this company. ISO 22301:2019, edition 2, publication 2019-10, 21 pages; official page says one amendment, ISO 22301:2019/Amd 1:2024, “Climate action changes.” Current 2019 text is at stage 90.92 (to be revised); successor ISO/CD 22301 is under development. Full base standard was not acquired.
- Captured text/facts: official preview describes a BCMS for planning, implementation, operation, monitoring, review, maintenance and continual improvement so an organization can protect against and recover from disruption.
- Free operational companion: CISA's [CRR Service Continuity Resource Guide, Volume 6, version 1.1](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/c3vp/crr_resources_guides/CRR_Resource_Guide-SC.pdf) was downloaded from the official URL and retained as `cisa-service-continuity-resource-guide-vol6-v1.1.pdf`; SHA-256 `1d3c5e18955297740a82a8b5f7d8d7a533b2ad46247ccbd285d2f088023b8632`. It is a 2016 CISA-hosted guide credited to Carnegie Mellon University, not ISO 22301 and not a substitute for its requirements.
- Useful pre-interview questions: Which critical services/processes and dependencies must continue? What are tolerable outage/data-loss limits, recovery priorities, workarounds, decision authorities, communications channels and suppliers? When was continuity last exercised, what failed, and who owns corrective action? Keep recovery objectives as unselected questions until Carlos and the relevant owners supply facts.
